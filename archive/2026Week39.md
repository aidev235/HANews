## 1. Sharp Higher Integrability Theory, Part I: The $L^p$-norm of the Beurling--Ahlfors transform

Authors: Andrea Agazzi, Kari Astala, Giuseppe Bruno, Gabriele Cassese, Hang Chang, Daniel Faraco, André Guerra, Bernd Kirchheim, Aleksis Koski, Jan Kristensen, Federico Pasqualotto, István Prause, Riccardo Tione, Vladimír Šverák, László Székelyhidi. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28311v1), submitted September 23, 2026, 10:55 CDT.

The planar transform has norm $p^*-1$, with $p^*=\max\{p,p/(p-1)\}$. The manuscript’s central theorem is quasiconvexity of a piecewise matrix integrand: it equals the determinant in the operator-norm unit ball and a convex expression outside it. An established implication then gives quasiconvexity of the Burkholder integrands and the sharp transform bound.

The proof uses a constrained Lipschitz replacement of one component of a periodic perturbation. A pointwise representation of the integrand, together with the determinant’s null-Lagrangian property, converts this replacement into the required averaged inequality. Thus the paper supplies a variational route to a sharp Fourier-multiplier constant, rather than merely improving an existing numerical estimate.

The significance is the proposed resolution of the planar norm problem and its connection to sharp quasiconformal higher integrability. The authors explicitly label the manuscript preliminary. Extensions to forms and higher-dimensional applications are announced for subsequent papers; they should not be counted as established results of this short first installment.

## 2. A Szemerédi-Trotter Theorem in Arbitrary Fields

Authors: Mark Lewko. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27023v1), submitted September 22, 2026, 15:08 CDT.

For $m$ points and $n$ lines, the claimed incidence bound is
$$I(P,L)\lesssim(mn)^{2/3}+m+n+mn/p,$$
with the last term absent in characteristic zero. The characteristic term is essential: arbitrary fields include configurations supported on finite subfields, so one cannot simply transplant the real bound at every scale.

The polynomial argument encodes input lines in an auxiliary polynomial with a slope variable. Incidences force high root multiplicities on parallel test lines over a rational-function extension; algebraic multiplicity counting takes the place of real topological cells. Degree restrictions in positive characteristic account for the extra term.

For harmonic analysis, v1 gives $L^2\to L^r$ paraboloid extension over prime fields with $p\equiv3\pmod4$ for $r>10/3$. Its extractor application has entropy rate greater than $3/8$. The current abstract advertises a later $1/3$ improvement, but v2 was submitted September 28 CDT, outside this issue. These are preprint claims; neither an extension endpoint nor an independent proof audit is supplied here.

## 3. Establishing the Polynomial Wolff Axioms for $\delta$-Separated $\delta$-Tubes With #o-minimality

Authors: Gal Binyamini, Yuval Salant. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24135v1), submitted September 21, 2026, 00:41 CDT.

Let $\mathbb T$ be direction-$\delta$-separated unit-length $\delta$-tubes, each meeting a semialgebraic set $S$ in at least a proportion $\lambda$. The result gives
$$|S|\gtrsim \operatorname{poly}_n(E)^{-1}\#\mathbb T\,\lambda^n\delta^{n-1},$$
where $E$ is the complexity, with no extra $\delta^\varepsilon$ loss. A corresponding algebraic-variety neighborhood bound has tube count at most $\operatorname{poly}_n(D)\delta^{1-k}$ for a $k$-dimensional variety of degree $D$.

The proof works directly with definable sets and quantitative o-minimal complexity. This avoids approximating a parametrization by a polynomial, while a more precise geometric selection removes an additional logarithmic pigeonholing loss. The general #o-minimal setting records polynomial dependence on degree at fixed format.

The immediate value is an exact geometric input for polynomial-partitioning and Kakeya arguments. It does not by itself control every configuration required for the Kakeya conjecture. Authors: Mingfeng Chen. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32421v1), submitted September 26, 2026, 04:48 CDT.

Squaring an input coordinate turns the relevant phase into a sum of one-variable quadratics. This would complete the conjectured multiparameter maximal range. The theorem’s measure convention and strict inequality are essential. Authors: Inbo Gottlieb Fenves, Jiahao Tan. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32090v1), submitted September 25, 2026, 18:54 CDT.

## 6. The Solution to Agmon's Conjecture for the polylaplacian

Authors: Artur Andrade, Dorina Mitrea, Irina Mitrea, Marius Mitrea. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25401v1), submitted September 21, 2026, 15:43 CDT.

For $\Delta^m$ in $\mathbb R^n$, the paper constructs the analogue of a harmonic double-layer representation acting on compatible arrays of boundary derivatives. It establishes existence, uniqueness, nontangential control and representation formulas for Dirichlet data, together with corresponding regularity results.

The main theorem assumes an Ahlfors regular domain whose outward normal has sufficiently small BMO norm. Boundary data belong to a specified hierarchy of spaces supporting the necessary maximal and singular-integral estimates. Whitney arrays encode compatibility between the boundary derivatives; matrix-valued multi-layer operators provide the new potential-theoretic machinery and invertibility mechanism.

The development realizes Agmon’s proposed higher-order potential approach for arbitrary polyharmonic order and Euclidean dimension. It also illustrates how quantitative boundary geometry can replace conventional smoothness assumptions. The conclusion is not unrestricted solvability on every rough domain, nor a classification of all higher-order elliptic systems. Those distinctions are material to interpreting the title and the scope of the preprint.

Every bounded stable entire solution of the three-dimensional Allen–Cahn equation is one-dimensional; apart from the constant states, it is a translated and rotated heteroclinic profile. The resulting classification implies the monotone four-dimensional case of De Giorgi’s conjecture.

The key step converts semilinear stability into geometric stability of the zero set while controlling interactions between transition layers. ## 2. Semifields in prime dimensions and counterexamples to Kaplansky's conjecture

Authors: Gábor P. Nagy, Yue Zhou. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32651v1), submitted September 26, 2026, 09:09 CDT.

Nagy and Zhou construct nonassociative finite division algebras that contradict the proposed eventual classification in dimension five. Their family exists for $q\equiv1\pmod3$ and $n\ge5$ with $\gcd(n,6)=1$, and its five-dimensional specialization works over arbitrarily large finite base fields.

The construction must establish both the division property and non-isotopy to known field and generalized twisted-field families. The authors use multiplication-matrix ranks at determinant vertices, together with fields of definition of determinant components, as finer invariants than nucleus sizes. ## 3. Dictators are most informative

Authors: Vu Khac Ky, Tuan Tran. Source and event: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24184v1), submitted September 21, 2026, 01:56 CDT.

For a uniform Boolean input $X$ and its independently corrupted binary-channel observation $Y$, Ky and Tran prove that no one-bit Boolean summary carries more mutual information about $Y$ than a coordinate function. In bits, the upper bound is $1-H_2(p)$ for crossover probability $p$.

Their proof separates functions near dictators from functions whose dependence is spread across coordinates. Local conditional-entropy comparisons treat the first class; entropy production along the noise semigroup and spectral estimates treat the second. Explicit scalar inequalities complete the comparison across noise levels.

An [independent same-week proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24931v1) by Chen, Gohari, Javanmard, Lin, Mirrokni, Nair and Woodruff uses a differential-equation/Bellman approach with computational verification records. A [stability companion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31797v1) quantifies closeness to dictators for near-extremizers. These overlapping contributions are grouped in one entry. Independent proofs strengthen the evidence, but this report has neither rerun their certificates nor verified every proof step; arXiv publication is not peer review.
