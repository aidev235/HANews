## 1. Curved Kakeya problems and the projective geometry of paths

**Authors:** Shaoming Guo, Larry Guth, Arian Nadjimzadah, Minxing Shen, Ruixiang Zhang
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2607.27629v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27629v1), new preprint submitted July 30, 2026.
**Topics:** curved Kakeya sets, oscillatory integrals, spray geometry, projective geometry, incidence geometry.

**What happened.** The preprint introduces a framework for curved Kakeya problems that includes curve families arising from Hörmander-type oscillatory integrals. It associates a spray geometry to a family of curves and characterizes the persistence of the incidence structure behind Wolff's hairbrush argument: in the authors' formulation, the required supply of totally geodesic surfaces is equivalent to projective flatness of the spray. Inside that class, Bourgain's condition yields a dichotomy between direction-equivalence to a Bochner–Riesz-type line family satisfying Katz–Wolff geometry and two-dimensional Kakeya sets supported on totally geodesic surfaces. A semi-algebraic result in three dimensions further relates dimension-two compression to containment in a surface.

**Assessment (interpretation).** The work is notable less as one isolated estimate than as a structural dictionary connecting variable-coefficient Kakeya geometry, projective invariants, and Euclidean incidence mechanisms. It does not claim to settle the general curved Kakeya problem. The item is an unrefereed preprint; the report verifies its date and stated results, not the proof.

## 2. The Endpoint Eigenfunction Bound for the Hermite Operator in Two Dimensions

**Authors:** Eunhee Jeong, Sanghyuk Lee, Jaehyeon Ryu
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2607.25859v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25859v1), new preprint submitted July 28, 2026.
**Topics:** Hermite operator, spectral projections, endpoint eigenfunction bounds, multiscale analysis, almost orthogonality.

**What happened.** The authors establish the optimal endpoint estimate
$L^2(\mathbb{R}^2)\to L^{10/3}(\mathbb{R}^2)$ for Hermite spectral projections. Their abstract states that, together with their earlier higher-dimensional endpoint work, this completes the optimal $L^2\to L^q$ eigenfunction-bound problem for Hermite projections in every dimension. The proof described there uses a space-time multiscale decomposition relative to the degeneracy set, an asymmetric refinement on the input side, and almost orthogonality.

**Assessment (interpretation).** Closing the two-dimensional endpoint removes the remaining exceptional case in a sharp spectral-projection theory closely connected to restriction phenomena. The full significance depends on scrutiny of the endpoint argument; the source is a first-version, unrefereed preprint.

## 3. The HRT Conjecture for Symmetric Configurations and Real-Valued Functions

**Authors:** Shuang Guan, Kasso A. Okoudjou
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2607.26878v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26878v1), new preprint submitted July 29, 2026.
**Topics:** time-frequency shifts, HRT conjecture, linear independence, irrational rotations, trigonometric polynomials.

**What happened.** The preprint proves the Heil–Ramanathan–Topiwala conjecture for an infinite family of symmetric $(2n+1,2)$ configurations and arbitrary generators in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, more generally allowing commensurable spacings among the collinear points. As a consequence, it establishes the conjecture for every configuration of four distinct points when the generating function is real-valued. The stated proof reduces the problem to products of trigonometric polynomials and estimates along irrational-rotation orbits.

**Assessment (interpretation).** This is a substantial advance on a basic time-frequency linear-independence problem: it enlarges the configuration classes known for arbitrary $L^2$ generators and gives a clean four-point theorem under a real-valued hypothesis. It does not settle the four-point problem for general complex-valued generators, much less the full HRT conjecture. The source is unrefereed.

## 4. Local smoothing for rough wave equations

**Authors:** Jan Rozendaal, Robert Schippa
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.01440v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01440v1), new preprint submitted August 2, 2026.
**Topics:** local smoothing, rough metrics, bilinear restriction, decoupling, wave equations.

**What happened.** The authors prove local-smoothing estimates for wave equations with $C^r$ coefficients using bilinear restriction estimates adapted to rough propagation. In dimensions two and three, their $C^{1,1}$ theory recovers the sharp $L^p$–$L^q$ local-smoothing bounds previously available for smooth coefficients. The preprint derives these from stronger $L^p$–$L^q$ decoupling inequalities with endpoint derivative loss and also obtains nontrivial $L^p$–$L^p$ local smoothing.

**Assessment (interpretation).** The main connection is between rough-coefficient wave-packet geometry and restriction/decoupling technology: the result tests how much of the smooth Fourier-integral-operator theory survives near the natural low-regularity threshold. The abstract's sharp recovery is limited to the stated dimensions and coefficient class. This is an unrefereed first version.

## 5. Almost everywhere convergence of Mock Fourier series for the standard middle-fourth Cantor measure with respect to its canonical spectrum

**Authors:** Min-Wei Tang, Zhi-Yi Wu
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2607.27796v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27796v1), new preprint submitted July 30, 2026.
**Topics:** spectral measures, singular continuous measures, maximal partial sums, almost-everywhere convergence, Cantor measures.

**What happened.** For the Jorgensen–Pedersen middle-fourth Cantor measure $\mu_{1/4}$ and its canonical spectrum $\Lambda_4$, the authors prove that the maximal operator of Mock Fourier partial sums is of weak type $(1,1)$. They consequently obtain $S_n f\to f$ almost everywhere with respect to $\mu_{1/4}$ for every $f\in L^1(\mu_{1/4})$. The abstract identifies this as the solution of a long-standing problem posed by Strichartz.

**Assessment (interpretation).** This supplies an endpoint maximal theorem—and hence a convergence theorem—in a genuinely singular spectral-measure setting, where the geometry and frequency set differ sharply from classical Lebesgue Fourier series. The claim should not be read as a general theorem for arbitrary spectral measures. The source is an unrefereed preprint.

## 3. Quantum Steenrod powers and Hamiltonian maps

**Authors:** Shaoyun Bai, Egor Shelukhin, Nicholas Wilkins, Guangbo Xu
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2607.25960v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25960v1), new preprint submitted July 28, 2026.
**Topics:** Hamiltonian dynamics, symplectic topology, quantum Steenrod operations, Floer homology, periodic points.

**What happened.** Using integral Hamiltonian Floer theory, an equivariant Künneth isomorphism, and quantitative analysis of quantum power maps, the authors derive several results for closed symplectic manifolds. Among them, a manifold admitting a Hamiltonian pseudo-rotation or a finite-order Hamiltonian diffeomorphism is shown to be geometrically uniruled, resolving a variant of a problem in McDuff–Salamon's list. The paper also gives new hypotheses forcing infinitely many simple periodic points, including a non-torsion periodic-orbit class and the presence of a symplectically degenerate maximum.

**Assessment (interpretation).** The work links quantum operations to concrete rigidity and orbit-growth conclusions in Hamiltonian dynamics, advancing several strands of the Conley-conjecture program at once. The exact hypotheses matter, and the paper does not assert the unrestricted Hofer–Zehnder or Conley conjectures. It is an unrefereed first version.
