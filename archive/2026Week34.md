## 1. Linear dependence of time-frequency shifts of a Schwartz function

**Authors:** Markus Faulhuber, Philipp Petersen, Jordy Timo van Velthoven, Felix Voigtlaender
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.05044v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05044v2), substantial revision posted August 18, 2026.
**Topics:** HRT conjecture, Gabor systems, Weyl operators, vector Zak transform, Schwartz eigenfunctions.

**Result and setting.** The first version constructed a Schwartz function with 12 distinct time-frequency shifts that are linearly dependent, disproving the Heil--Ramanathan--Topiwala conjecture through an explicit interval-certified construction. Version 2 adds a separate qualitative existence theorem whose proof is purely analytic. This does not reduce the number 12; it makes the existence of dependence independent of numerical certification and exposes a reusable mechanism.

**Main ideas.** The authors recast linear dependence as an eigenvalue equation $Pf=cf$ for a finite Weyl polynomial. A two-component vector Zak transform turns $P$ into multiplication by a $2\times2$ matrix symbol. They construct a smooth unit vector field $\chi$, use the rank-one field $B=\chi\chi^*$ and finitely many Fourier modes, and solve a scalar Fourier/cohomological equation producing a quasi-periodic vector Zak function $F$ that is an eigenvector. Inverting the transform gives the required Schwartz eigenfunction. The vector-valued step is essential: the manuscript proves that the analogous scalar Zak construction must vanish.

**Importance and connections.** The new proof makes the HRT failure look geometric and operator-theoretic rather than accidental. It also separates two achievements: a conceptual existence mechanism and a concrete 12-shift certificate. That distinction matters for future attempts to classify which configurations still force independence or to determine the true minimum size.

**Provenance and caveats.** The authors disclose that GPT-5.6 Pro proposed the counterexample and proof strategy; human collaborators report finding and repairing a nontrivial error, independently checking the mathematical steps, and rewriting the final proof. [Terence Tao's earlier discussion](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/08/06/a-partial-digestion-of-the-hrt-counterexample/) gives informed outside scrutiny and anticipated that a qualitative proof might avoid numerics, but it is not a referee report. Version 2 remains an author preprint.

## 2. A dimension-free weak-type $(1,1)$ bound for the vector Riesz transform on $\mathbb{R}^n$

**Authors:** Yuyuan Ouyang, Daniel Spector, Cody B. Stockdale
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.18068v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18068v1), new preprint submitted August 18, 2026.
**Topics:** vector Riesz transform, weak type $(1,1)$, fractional Laplacian, obstacle problems, dimension-free estimates.

**Result and setting.** For the full vector Riesz transform $R=(R_1,\ldots,R_n)$, the paper proves
$$
|\{x:|Rf(x)|>\lambda\}|\le \frac{2}{\lambda}\|f\|_1
$$
with a constant independent of $n$. This answers the dimension-free endpoint question posed by Stein at the 1986 ICM. The statement is an upper bound by 2; it does not identify the exact best constant in each dimension.

**Main ideas.** Instead of trying to extrapolate dimension-free control from the separate scalar transforms, the authors decompose the input through an obstacle problem for $(-\Delta)^{1/2}$. An associated Lewy--Stampacchia inequality on an unbounded domain controls the measure/density created by the decomposition. The harmonic-extension and gradient structure then identifies the controlled term with the vector Riesz transform, while Chebyshev and $L^2$ estimates close the weak endpoint bound.

**Importance and connections.** Endpoint bounds normally deteriorate with the number of components, so an absolute constant is a strong structural statement. The obstacle method also travels: a same-week paper extends it to stratified Lie groups using heat semigroups and Dirichlet forms. That rapid generalization suggests potential theory, rather than Euclidean Fourier symmetry, is the durable part of the argument.

**Provenance and caveats.** The preprint describes extensive model-assisted exploration. An LLM-generated torus/transference route helped lead the authors to a simpler Euclidean proof, and Claude supplied the basis of the eventual obstacle idea; the authors say they verified and rewrote the proof. There is not yet an independent audit or peer review.

## 3. Endpoint orthonormal Strichartz estimates

**Authors:** Sonae Hadama, Shunya Toyoshima
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.21823v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21823v1), new preprint submitted August 22, 2026.
**Topics:** orthonormal systems, Strichartz estimates, Keel--Tao framework, Schatten--Lorentz ideals, free transport.

**Result and setting.** The classical Keel--Tao theorem derives single-function Strichartz estimates from energy and dispersive bounds for an abstract propagator $U(t)$. Hadama and Toyoshima prove the corresponding orthonormal-family estimate under precisely those hypotheses. For Schrödinger evolution in $d\ge2$, this resolves the endpoint conjecture raised by Frank, Lewin, Lieb, and Seiringer; by duality it also answers the endpoint free-transport conjecture of Bennett, Bez, Gutiérrez, and Lee.

**Main ideas.** Duality converts the orthonormal estimate into a weak Schatten--Lorentz bound for
$$
\int U(t)^*V(t)U(t)\,dt,
$$
or equivalently an eigenvalue-counting problem for a weighted $UU^*$ operator. After dyadicizing the potential, the authors split time interactions into near and far pieces at a scale adapted to the amplitude pair. Far intervals exploit dispersion through Hilbert--Schmidt control. Near intervals use energy estimates, spectral dyadic bands, and regrouping by relative amplitude and time indices; a selected spectral scale forces geometric decay of high-band eigenvalue counts and closes the endpoint summation.

**Importance and connections.** The argument is not tied to a Fourier formula for the Schrödinger kernel, so it applies to every evolution satisfying the Keel--Tao assumptions. The same machinery yields a refinement for a single function, while interpolation recovers strong estimates away from the endpoint. This strengthens the analytical bridge among dispersive PDE, kinetic transport, many-body density estimates, and operator ideals.

**Caveats.** The endpoint is delicate: the fundamental estimate is weak/Lorentz, with strong interior bounds obtained subsequently. The restricted endpoint fails in dimension one, so the Schrödinger consequence correctly starts at $d=2$. The manuscript is a first, unrefereed version.

## 4. Mixed-norm Brascamp--Lieb inequalities

**Author:** Ruixiang Zhang
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.17952v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17952v2), new preprint submitted August 18 and revised August 19, 2026.
**Topics:** Brascamp--Lieb inequalities, mixed norms, multilinear operators, tensor powers, Kakeya.

**Result and setting.** Classical Brascamp--Lieb theory controls multilinear integral forms using a single exponent family and linear dimension inequalities. Zhang introduces forms with separate mixed norms in two groups of variables and identifies a $q<2$ regime not captured by that theory. A result of Christ becomes the first nontrivial example, and the paper reaches an endpoint that ordinary interpolation misses.

**Main ideas.** The proof first establishes a discrete restricted weak-type inequality. Dyadic pigeonholing of the inputs produces logarithmic losses, which would prevent the desired endpoint. The crucial maneuver takes an $N$-fold Cartesian tensor product, applies the lossy estimate in the product space, and then takes $N$th roots. The logarithmic factor becomes negligible as $N\to\infty$, upgrading the bound to the genuine mixed-norm endpoint and slightly sharpening Christ's argument.

**Importance and connections.** Tensor powers are a familiar loss-removal device, but here they unlock a new inequality landscape. The paper compares that landscape with multilinear Kakeya and identifies difficulties absent from classical Brascamp--Lieb theory—particularly the lack of an adequate perturbative theorem. These obstructions may be as informative as the positive estimate for future restriction work.

**Caveats.** The article provides model theorems and a framework, not a new Kakeya dimension or restriction exponent. The absence of a perturbed Brascamp--Lieb analogue means the usual route from multilinear inequalities to Kakeya does not automatically transfer. The work remains unrefereed.

## 5. Sharp Bilinear Decoupling under Shell-Type Restrictions and Applications

**Author:** Jiajun Wang
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.19531v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19531v1), new preprint submitted August 19, 2026.
**Topics:** bilinear decoupling, shell Fourier support, cap--plate geometry, Strichartz estimates, Zakharov systems.

**Result and setting.** The paper asks how bilinear decoupling improves when the Fourier supports lie on thin shells rather than thick annuli. It identifies the sharp coefficient and a transition between the regimes $N_1\ge N_2^2$ and $N_1\le N_2^2$. Matching configurations show that the coefficient cannot be improved in the stated shell model.

**Main ideas.** A lossless $L^2$ cap--plate decomposition retains the anisotropic geometry of shell support. One factor is handled by a one-direction linear lifting followed by localized shell-type linear decoupling; complementary localization controls the other. Polar, equatorial, and generic configurations are separated, as are the two frequency regimes around $N_1=N_2^2$. Frequency and modulation decompositions plus interpolation then turn the decoupling statement into bilinear spacetime estimates.

**Importance and connections.** The applications include bilinear Strichartz inequalities, estimates for toral eigenfunctions, additive energy of lattice points on spheres, and a high--low trilinear estimate giving separated nonlinear smoothing for the periodic Zakharov system. The collection demonstrates that using the exact codimension-one support, rather than treating it as a thick region, can materially sharpen both harmonic-analytic and PDE estimates.

**Caveats.** The phase transition and sharp examples belong to the specified shell restrictions; they should not be read as a universal bilinear decoupling theorem for arbitrary curved supports. This is an unrefereed first version.

## 6. Global solutions for 1D cubic defocusing dispersive equations, Part V: low regularity NLS

**Authors:** Mihaela Ifrim, Ryan Martinez, Daniel Tataru
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.17156v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17156v1), new preprint submitted August 17, 2026.
**Topics:** low-regularity NLS, interaction Morawetz estimates, frequency envelopes, implicit normal forms, global dispersive bounds.

**Result and setting.** The authors study a broad class of one-dimensional cubic defocusing dispersive equations, including but not limited to integrable cubic NLS. For small initial data they obtain global solutions and dispersive bounds in $H^s$ for every $s>-1/2$, approaching the scaling-critical threshold from above. The estimates include $L^6_{t,x}$ Strichartz and bilinear $L^2_{t,x}$ control that are new even for classical defocusing cubic NLS at negative regularity.

**Main ideas.** Cubic interactions are separated into balanced and unbalanced frequency configurations. For the balanced part, localized mass and momentum density--flux identities receive quartic corrections, and diagonal/transversal interaction Morawetz functionals provide the spacetime bounds. Asymmetric dyadic frequency envelopes suppress high-to-low transfer. The nonperturbative high--high--medium-to-low cascade is removed by a finite-order implicit normal-form relation
$$
v=u+\sum_{n=1}^{N}B_{2n+1}(v,\ldots,v),
$$
which resums successive corrections; choosing $N$ so that $s>-2N/(2N+1)$ reaches every $s>-1/2$.

**Importance and connections.** Previous negative-regularity results for classical cubic NLS could exploit complete integrability. This method instead uses conservation structure, multilinear harmonic analysis, and renormalization robust enough for nonintegrable symbols. A joint bootstrap propagates energy, Strichartz, and bilinear bounds; for classical cubic NLS, scaling promotes the dispersive estimates to large data.

**Caveats.** The endpoint $s=-1/2$ remains open. Smooth symbol assumptions and a defocusing/conservative structure are essential. The general theorem is small-data; the large-data extension by scaling is special to classical cubic NLS. The long manuscript is not yet peer reviewed.

## 1. Entry growth in Gaussian elimination

**Authors:** Rikhav Shah, John Urschel
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.19189v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19189v1), new preprint submitted August 19, 2026; a v2 also appeared in-window.
**Topics:** numerical stability, Gaussian elimination, pivoting, growth factors, complexity.

**What happened.** Intermediate matrix entries can grow dramatically during elimination even when the final linear system is well conditioned; this growth factor is central to classical backward-error analysis. Shah and Urschel determine the worst-case order for complete and rook pivoting as quasipolynomial, closing a long gap between known bounds. **Methods and significance.** The paper combines explicit sparse constructions, projection determinantal point processes, Hadamard-matrix identities, and probabilistic block constructions. The comparison among pivoting rules is as important as any one bound: complete and rook pivoting behave qualitatively better in the worst case, while randomization alone does not eliminate pathological partial-pivoting growth.

**Caveats and version control.** These are worst-case theorems, not evidence that routine numerical workloads are broadly unstable. The v1 manuscript says it was prepared under a short deadline and would be revised; several sections involved substantial human--AI collaboration, with the authors taking responsibility for the proofs. A post-window v3 strengthens the hardness label from NP-hard to NP-complete and modifies hypotheses, so this issue deliberately reports only the in-window v1 claims.

## 2. On Chern's conjecture for minimal submanifolds of the sphere

**Authors:** Benjy Firester, Raphael Tsiamis
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.18074v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18074v1), new preprint submitted August 18, 2026.
**Topics:** minimal submanifolds, second fundamental form, scalar curvature, homogeneous geometry.

**What happened.** The Chern--do Carmo--Kobayashi discreteness conjecture predicts that a closed minimal submanifold of a sphere with constant squared second-fundamental form $|A|^2$ should realize only a discrete set of values. Using the Gauss equation, this is equivalently a scalar-curvature rigidity statement. Firester and Tsiamis construct countable families of closed embedded examples whose constant $|A|^2$ values are dense in a bounded interval, disproving discreteness for all $n\ge3,m\ge4$ and for even $n\ge4,m\ge3$ in their notation.

**Main idea and significance.** Homogeneous minimal embeddings arising from isoparametric hypersurfaces and torus actions supply continuously varying geometric data; suitable rational parameters yield the countable dense families. Thus the expected rigidity fails not through an isolated exception but through systematic higher-codimension abundance.

**Caveats.** The title's “Chern's conjecture” should not be read as closing every case. Important codimension-one and codimension-two regimes remain outside the construction. The argument is concise—only five pages—but currently supported by an unrefereed author manuscript.

## 3. Banach's Isometric Conjecture over the Complex Field

**Authors:** Antonio Acuaviva, Tomasz Kania
**Source/event:** [arXiv:2608.18257v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18257v1), new preprint submitted August 18, 2026.
**Topics:** normed spaces, mutually isometric sections, Hermitian norms, star bodies, bundle degree.

**What happened.** Banach's isometric problem asks whether a normed space whose fixed-dimensional subspaces are all mutually isometric must be Hilbertian. Following the just-prior resolution of the real case by Lu and Yang, Acuaviva and Kania prove the complex version: the norm must arise from a Hermitian inner product. They also obtain the quaternionic analogue and extensions to several broader topological-vector-space settings.

**Main idea and significance.** The authors first establish a real hyperplane theorem for star bodies without assuming convexity or central symmetry. Circled complex and quaternionic unit balls can then be analyzed through the bundle-degree mechanism introduced in the real solution. This completes a natural scalar-field picture and reinforces a striking principle: enough exact isometry among finite-dimensional sections forces an ellipsoidal norm.

**Caveats and provenance.** The proof is an extension of a breakthrough posted on August 13, outside this issue's window, rather than an independent solution of the real problem. The manuscript reports ChatGPT-5.6 Sol assistance with technical extension details; the authors remain responsible for the mathematical verification. It is not yet peer reviewed.
